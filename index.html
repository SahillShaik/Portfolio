Case Study · ADDIE

Technical Training Program: Jira for Agile Teams

A blended, multi-format curriculum designed to move Agile teams from inconsistent Jira usage to confident, standardized adoption.

Instructional DesignerSole ID — analysis to launch
8 weeksTimeline
Storyline, Rise, VyondTools used
Degreed / SumTotalLMS
4 formatsCourse + sim + video + job aid

Leadership had a visibility problem: sprint reports pulled from Jira were unreliable because teams didn't use the tool the same way — some skipped story points, others created undocumented custom issue types, and onboarding depended entirely on whichever teammate had time to explain it. A one-off live training session hadn't stuck, since there was no reference material to fall back on and it didn't reach distributed teams consistently. Rather than force one course to serve both brand-new hires and existing team members unlearning bad habits, the program was split into four connected pieces, each doing the job it's best suited for.

  • Rise 360 fundamentals microlearning — navigation, issue creation, board mechanics
  • Storyline 360 branching sprint-planning simulation — prioritization & capacity judgment calls
  • Short-form video tutorials — issue creation & sprint configuration walkthroughs
  • Workflow & reporting job aids, plus a Power BI adoption dashboard for team leads
Process — ADDIE
01Analyze

Interviewed 4 Scrum Masters and pulled Jira admin data to identify where teams diverged from standard practice. Found two distinct needs: brand-new users needed foundational navigation, while existing users needed a shared mental model for sprint-planning decisions — different problems, different formats.

02Design

Split the curriculum instead of forcing one course to do both jobs: a short Rise reference course for onboarding/navigation, and a Storyline branching scenario for the judgment-call skills (prioritization, capacity planning) a reference course can't teach. Mapped objectives to Bloom's Taxonomy so the Rise course stayed at remember/understand and the Storyline scenario targeted apply/analyze.

03Develop

Built full storyboards for both courses, then developed in Storyline 360 and Rise 360. Produced short Vyond/screen-capture video tutorials for the two most error-prone workflows (issue creation, sprint configuration). Created a one-page Jira conventions job aid for at-the-desk reference.

04Implement

Deployed via Degreed and SumTotal depending on business unit. Sequenced the Rise course first as a prerequisite, then the Storyline simulation, with the job aid linked from both.

05Evaluate

Tracked completion rates, quiz pass rates, and time-on-module in Degreed; built a Power BI dashboard so team leads could see adoption trends without pulling raw LMS exports. Used early quiz-item analytics to revise one confusing decision point in the Storyline scenario — learners were misreading the capacity meter — before wide rollout.

Curriculum Architecture — 4 connected pieces
RS

Rise 360 — Fundamentals

6 lessons, ~20–25 min, mobile-friendly. Designed as a living reference new hires reopen after onboarding week.

SL

Storyline 360 — Simulation

12–15 min branching scenario, SCORM 2004. Drag-and-drop capacity planning with differentiated feedback layers.

VT

Video Tutorials

Two 60–90s Vyond/screen-capture walkthroughs, linked from both courses and the job aid.

JA

Job Aids & Dashboard

One-page Jira conventions reference, plus a Power BI dashboard guide for team leads.

How success was measured
  • Completion & quiz pass-rate tracking in Degreed/SumTotal, broken out by team
  • Power BI dashboard giving team leads self-serve adoption visibility
  • Early-cohort analytics caught a confusing Storyline interaction before full rollout

"The biggest design decision was refusing to build one course to serve two different learner needs. Splitting them made both pieces sharper, and made the Rise course reusable long after the Storyline simulation had done its job."
